01 · THE PRODUCTA record, not a form

The wardrobe-app category asks for eight hours of photography, then charges you to see your own numbers — or stays free by earning when you buy more. Almari takes the opposite bet: everything the others charge for is free, the daily loop is two taps, and the record is never hostage.

The quiet features are the point: a wishlist with a seven-day cooling-off that waits silently and then asks once. A mending pile, because a torn shirt is neither clean nor dirty. Retirement instead of deletion, with history kept. A drawn technical flat for every piece without a photograph, so photo-free use looks intentional — nothing here ever asks who you are, only what the clothes are.

02 · THE PROOFEight worked wardrobes

The samples are the demo, the test fixtures, and the tutorial in one. Five are briefed the way a costume department is briefed — a wardrobe written as an idea and an arc, in period and in cloth, belonging to nobody.

The Candlelight Wardrobe

Kilkenny → the Continent → Wiltshire, 1760s

A tenant farmer's son gambles his way into an English estate; every coat is a receipt for a rung of the ladder. The ledger's cautionary tale — a court suit worn once, and a sale the bailiffs attended.

Four Nails Behind a Door

Mexico City, 1970

A live-in housekeeper's sixteen pieces, worn to the thread. The smallest closet carries the live cooling-off: a blouse being saved for, one week at a time. This wardrobe exists to prove the app is decent to somebody who owns almost nothing.

Salt and Indigo

An island near Busan → Osaka, 1933

A hanbok becomes a Japanese housedress one garment at a time. Nothing retires here, and that is the point — what is mended is not poor. Her mother's silk jeogori is kept, not worn.

Bright, Then Earth

Lagos → Nsukka → Umuahia, 1966

Forty-eight pieces becoming twelve in eighteen months. Retirement is the story: what is given away is given away whole. One strand of coral remains, and the wishlist keeps a bolt of george, for after.

Whites and Blacks

Chicago, now

A chef who decided once and bought the same tee nine times — the hardest closet a wear-tracker can be given. His overcoat sat on the wishlist through two winters and was bought the week the review ran.

And three of today

Kochi · Mumbai · New Delhi

Three contemporary Indian wardrobes — festival drapes, client weeks, wedding seasons — sharing a rail, lending and borrowing with provenance kept. The ceremony features no competitor carries.

No persona is named after a character or a performer, and none will ever be: the wardrobe is briefed, never borrowed. The rule is kept in the codebase beside the wardrobes themselves.

03 · THE MODELPriced once, honestly

The web app is free — everything, permanently. The native apps for iOS and Android will cost a single small price, in the tradition of tools that are bought once and kept. No subscription, no tiers, no ads, nothing sold but the app itself.

India
₹299one time, on either store
Elsewhere
$4.99one time — a price this category has sustained for over a decade
The web
₹0forever, with nothing withheld

This model is chosen because it is the only one that cannot bend the product. A subscription needs retention mechanics; an ad model needs attention; a commerce model needs you to buy more clothes. A one-time price needs only that the app be worth it on the day you buy it — and it aligns the company with the product's own promise of permanence.

04 · THE PLANTwelve months, two gates

An Indian private limited company, incorporated for three reasons a repository cannot do: sign contracts, sell in two stores, and hold the mark. The critical path runs through exactly two external gates — trademark clearance and App Review. Everything else is work.

  • Months 1–2
    The company, and the name examined.

    Incorporation via SPICe+, then DPIIT recognition the week the certificate arrives. An attorney clearance search on the mark before a rupee of brand spend — the field is crowded, and the plan prices a rename now at a fraction of a rename after launch. Hardening ships meanwhile: the sample wardrobes photographed from open-licence museum collections.

  • Month 3
    The mark filed, the fitting built.

    Trademark filed in classes 9 and 42, word and device, with expedited examination — filed the moment clearance allows, never before. The tutorial built to its written spec: one welcome sheet, five coach marks, then silence.

  • Months 3–6
    The port.

    The native wrap: the same app, storage moved early to durable native ground through the migration layer that already round-trips losslessly — so the alpha tests what will actually ship — with camera, share sheet, haptics, and an optional Face ID lock. Internal TestFlight from month five; moderated alpha sessions and the first diary study follow.

  • Months 4–9
    Grant season, alongside.

    Applications to the Startup India Seed Fund and MeitY GENESIS against a working beta — non-dilutive money fits a company shaped like this one; venture money is a named fork, off by default. The state grant window falls at month twelve and is planned for, not hoped for.

  • Months 6–9
    True offline, and the open beta.

    The service worker finished; sync-you-own file sync — a file the person controls, no account anywhere. Play open track and TestFlight external. Because the app will never phone home, testing is moderated sessions, diary studies, and the support mail — measurement by consent, which is slower and honest. At month eight, a named gate: the opposition window on the mark is checked before any launch spend.

  • Months 9–10
    The stores.

    Submission, one budgeted review cycle, launch on both platforms. First revenue. The web app unchanged, still free, still the whole product.

  • Months 11–12
    The quiet season.

    Nano and micro collaborations — honest wardrobe audits, no promo codes, because there is nothing to code. Press on the craft: ownership, the thirty-wears arithmetic, and the hand-drawn plates. Then a raise/no-raise decision on stated gates, with no-raise as the default.

The same plan, as assignable work with owners and dates, lives in the workroom.

What the AI does, and does not do

The app was built with Claude Code and the port will be too — that is the workshop, not the product. In the product there is no Almari-operated AI at all: the photo-intake feature hands a prompt to a vision model the person already uses, on their own account, and the native apps may add a bring-your-own-key convenience. Cataloguing a hundred-piece wardrobe on a person's own key costs them roughly the price of a cup of chai. Nothing leaves the device unless the person connects a key of their own — and then photos go only to the provider they chose, on their own account. Almari never sees them, because there is no company server to see them with.

The tutorial, planned to the word

One welcome sheet — "The first fitting" — then five dotted coach marks in the manner of a tailor's annotations: the two-tap log, the drawn flats, the falling cost-per-wear, the wishlist that waits, the export that is always yours. A step count, never a progress bar. No confetti, no streaks, and at the end, deliberately, nothing.

06 · THE CONTRACTWhat this company will never do

Set by a focus group before the first commit, kept by a linter, and restated here so no future conversation reopens them.

  • No accounts, no cloud sync
  • No telemetry, no analytics SDKs
  • No ads
  • No shop links or affiliate codes
  • No subscriptions
  • No item caps
  • No notifications
  • No badges, streaks, or confetti
  • No shame mechanics
  • No gendered anything
  • No required field that erases someone
  • No export that loses a field

A feature that talks you out of buying cannot profit from buying. That sentence is load-bearing: it is why the wishlist can be trusted, and why the company is small on purpose.
