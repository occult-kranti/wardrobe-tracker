# The instruments ledger

**Date:** 13 August 2026 · **For:** the meetings of 15 and 22 August
**Status:** the paperwork plan of record. Corrects `docs/29` §3.3 on one point that matters.

Four counsel were briefed independently — a corporate/equity specialist, an IP
chain-of-title specialist, a litigator asked only "what does this look like in
a courtroom", and a company secretary asked only "what gets signed, on what,
in what order". This is their work reduced to a ledger: every instrument, who
signs it, what it must say, when, and what happens if it is late.

Two things make it urgent rather than administrative. **Nothing here can be
retrofitted cheaply** — the Articles are set at the filing, entrenchment closes
at the filing, and an assignment signed after the code is written is an
assignment that may never have been needed by the person who already owns the
code. And **the roster changed on 13 August**, which changes who signs what.

---

## What the roster change did to this ledger

The research assumed Kunjal, Nimesh and Raksha were three developer-joiners who
would all need identical IP assignments. That is now wrong in two directions,
and both directions are improvements — provided the paperwork follows.

| Person | Role now | What that changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hrudayangam** | Co-founder | Full founder set. Unchanged. |
| **Kunjal** | Co-founder | Full founder set — SHA, vesting, IP assignment, moonlighting warranty. The **only** joiner who now needs the developer treatment. |
| **Nimesh** | Independent Director | **No IP assignment for code, because he writes none.** But he gains a statutory apparatus of his own — and two bars that make the equity conversation different. |
| **Raksha** | Chartered Accountant | Depends entirely on one unanswered question: statutory auditor, or internal finance. The two are mutually exclusive and the ADT-1 filing forces the answer within 30 days of incorporation. |

**Note — the independent director bars.** Two rules make "Independent Director"
an expensive title if the intent was really "co-founder who advises":
`s.149(6)` requires no pecuniary relationship with the company beyond sitting
fees, and Schedule IV with `s.197(7)` bars stock options entirely. If Nimesh is
meant to hold equity or do work, this title actively prevents it, and a Pvt Ltd
is not required to have an independent director at all. The cheap version of
this conversation is one call to the CS before the 22nd. The expensive version
is discovering it during a diligence process.

**Note — the auditor conflict.** `s.141(3)` disqualifies from statutory audit
any officer or employee of the company, any partner or employee of one, and
anyone holding securities in it. So Raksha can sign the audit *or* hold shares
and a role — not both. Decide before ADT-1, because the filing is the decision
whether or not anyone treated it as one.

## The correction to `docs/29`

`docs/29` §3.3 says the compulsory-transfer call option is held by "the company
or the continuing founders". **Delete "the company."**

If the company is the buyer, the transaction *is* a buy-back, and `s.68` arrives
in full: it may be funded only out of free reserves, the securities premium
account, or a fresh issue — and a pre-revenue company carrying accumulated
losses has none of those. `s.66` reduction needs the NCLT. Since 1 October 2024
the tax position is worse again: `s.115QA` was withdrawn and the entire buy-back
consideration is deemed a dividend in the shareholder's hands under `s.2(22)(f)`,
taxed at slab rates with no deduction for cost of acquisition.

So the option must run to **Hrudayangam personally, with the other continuing
shareholders as alternates in proportion**, or to a nominee. Counsel called this
the single most common drafting error in Indian founder documents: it converts a
working instrument into a Section 68 problem at the exact moment you need it.

> **Note on A.3 — why it cannot wait.** `s.17` of the Copyright Act 1957 vests
> first ownership in the employer only for work made in the course of employment
> **under a contract of service**. A contractor owns their own output absent a
> written assignment, and there is no company to employ anyone until the
> Certificate of Incorporation issues. Anything Kunjal writes between 22 August
> and late September belongs to Kunjal personally.

> **Note on A.3 — the four traps that silently truncate a good deed.**
> `s.19(3)` the assignment must state the rights, the duration and the
> territory. `s.19(5)` no duration stated means five years — a 2026 deed quietly
> expires in 2031. `s.19(6)` no territory stated means India only, which is the
> wrong half of the map for a dollar-priced app. `s.19(4)` rights not exercised
> within a year may lapse. And `s.57` moral rights survive assignment and are
> not cleanly assignable on the Indian position — take the waiver, but do not
> let it be the only protection for the drawn plates.

> **Note on A.3 — the one line that must NOT appear.** No reversion of rights on
> cessation of service. Vesting belongs on the **shares**, never on the
> **assignment**. A conditional assignment makes the company's title to its only
> asset contingent, and every diligence process from a SISFS incubator onward
> will read that as owning nothing.

> **Note on B.3 and B.4 — why the filing is the deadline and not a milestone.**
> At the instant the Certificate of Incorporation issues, subscriber shares
> exist, in the numbers on the subscriber sheet, **under the Articles that were
> attached to the filing**. File on Table F with no SHA and you have created
> shares that are fully vested from minute one, with no call option, no transfer
> restriction beyond the bare `s.2(68)` minimum, and no reserved matters. Every
> retrofit from that point needs the consent of the person you would be
> restricting.

> **Note on entrenchment.** `s.5(3)` lets the Articles make some provisions
> alterable only on conditions stricter than a special resolution. Under
> `s.5(4)`, entrenchment can be introduced **at incorporation**, or afterwards in
> a private company **only by agreement of all the members**. Unanimity from four
> people who have since developed divergent interests is not a plan. The window
> closes at the filing.

> **Note on stamping.** An unstamped or under-stamped instrument is not merely
> untidy — under `s.35` of the Indian Stamp Act it is inadmissible in evidence
> until the deficit and a penalty of up to ten times the duty are paid. The
> penalty is cheap; the timing is not, because it surfaces during diligence,
> which is the one week you cannot afford a four-week Collector process.

## What it looks like when it goes wrong

The litigator was asked only this. Prices are defence costs, not damages.

| # | Scenario | Forum | Money | Months | The August fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "About 10%" promised verbally, claimed later | civil suit or arbitration | ₹3–15L defending; the shares if allotted | 6–36 | **A.2**, signed at the table |
| 2 | Kunjal's employer claims the work | declaratory suit, or a stalled deal | ₹5–50L, or the deal | 3–24 | **A.4** and **A.5**; read his contract first |
| 3 | Founder deed unstamped at diligence | Collector, `s.33`/`s.40` | deficit + up to 10× penalty | 1–4, at the worst moment | **0.3** — e-stamp everything |
| 4 | A paid clone of the public repo | platform form; a suit only in theory | ₹0–50K vs ₹15–50L | days vs 12–24 | LICENSE file + **C.6** |
| 5 | No minutes of 22 August | nothing — it poisons the SHA instead | ₹2–5L of renegotiation | 2–3 | Circulate the note the same evening |
| 6 | The 2019 ALMARI sends a notice in launch week | injunction application; store dispute | several lakh + rebuilt listings | 1–120 | Brand-neutral package identifier; open the use-evidence file today |
| 7 | A migration destroys a wardrobe and she posts about it | District Consumer Commission, in **her** district | unpredictable; reputation dominates | 6–24 | Pre-migration snapshot; EULA capped at the purchase price |

> **Note on 5.** This is the cheapest one to prevent and the one most likely to
> happen. Four people leave a four-hour meeting with four different recollections
> of what was agreed about equity. Whoever chairs writes it down the same
> evening and circulates it; silence for 48 hours is acceptance. That note is
> what counsel drafts the SHA from.

> **Note on 7.** The forum matters more than the merits. A consumer complaint is
> filed where the *complainant* lives, is cheap for her and expensive for you,
> and does not care that the app is ₹499. The defence is the pre-migration
> snapshot in the shared layer, which is already the first task on the Tech
> Workbench.

## The five questions to put to counsel in writing

1. Does the call option in the SHA and the Articles run to **Hrudayangam and the
   continuing shareholders**, never the company, and is it replicated in the
   Articles rather than only in the SHA?
2. Given `V.B. Rangaraj`, which restrictions survive if they are in the SHA but
   *not* mirrored in the Articles — and confirm none of the ones we are relying
   on are in that category.
3. Is an Independent Director appropriate for a private limited company of this
   size, and what exactly does `s.149(6)` and Schedule IV cost Nimesh in terms
   of equity and paid work?
4. Statutory auditor or internal CA for Raksha — and what does the ADT-1 filing
   commit us to that we cannot undo?
5. Does the pre-incorporation assignment survive the gap between 22 August and
   incorporation without a company in existence, and is the power of attorney
   sufficient for the confirmatory deed?

## What is not settled

Stated plainly, because a ledger that hides its uncertainty is worse than none.

- **Moral rights under `s.57` are not cleanly waivable** on the Indian position.
  Standard practice takes the waiver anyway. Do not let it be the only
  protection for the artwork.
- **Market fee bands are unsourced.** ₹25,000–75,000 for the SHA is a spread,
  and the spread is quality. Get two written quotes.
- **The AI-generated work question is open.** Indian law has no settled position
  on authorship of AI-assisted output. The acknowledgment in A.6 assigns
  whatever rights exist; it cannot create rights that do not.
- **Contractor versus employee misclassification** was flagged and not resolved.
  It bears on `s.17` first ownership and on tax. Ask the CA alongside question 4.
